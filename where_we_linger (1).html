Afternoon Light
in Positano

May 18, 2026 • Italy • 6 Min Read

Positano doesn’t reveal itself immediately. It asks you to climb, to wander, and to stop for coffee far more often than you had planned. Arriving by sea in the mid-morning, the cliffside village appears as if carved from rose sandstone, climbing impossibly from the turquoise edge of the Tyrrhenian directly into the blue sky.

The scent of sea salt and crushed Meyer lemon leaves clings to every shadowed archway. While crowds gravitate toward the main beach by midday, the real magic of this place belongs to the quiet hours between three and six in the afternoon, when the sun dips behind the western ridge of Monte Faito and a softened, luminous veil of amber settles over the pastel façades.

The streets are steep and winding, the houses stacked like waves. Sit in the afternoon light; everything slows down. Linen dries on narrow wrought-iron balconies, the sea sparkles like polished glass, and life feels beautifully, effortlessly simple.

“To linger in Positano is to understand that beauty does not rush. It asks only for your full, unhurried attention.”

Founder & Landscape Architect

Hi, I’m Theresa.

I’m a landscape architect with a love for thoughtful travel, good food and places designed for people.

Where We Linger grew from the belief that every destination has a story — and the best way to discover it is by slowing down, noticing the light, and giving ourselves permission to sit at an outdoor table for hours.

I spend my days researching vernacular stone architecture, visiting family-run ceramic ateliers, and tasting pasta sauces in tiny Mediterranean villages.
